# CoSearch: Joint Training of Reasoning and Document Ranking via Reinforcement Learning for Agentic Search

**ArXiv ID:** [2604.17555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17555)  
**Published:** April 21, 2026  
**Authors:** Hansi Zeng et al. (Snap Research)  
**Institution:** Snap Inc.  
**Field:** Natural Language Processing / Information Retrieval  
**Code:** Available via paper (see Resources section)

---

## Executive Summary

CoSearch introduces a framework for *agentic search* that jointly trains a multi-step reasoning agent and a generative document ranker, using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Prior work treats the retrieval system as a fixed black box, optimizing only the reasoning agent. CoSearch identifies retrieval quality as the key bottleneck—a gap of up to **+26.8% relative F1** between oracle and fixed retrieval—and closes that gap by co-evolving both reasoning and retrieval through shared RL signal, achieving consistent improvements across seven single-hop and multi-hop QA benchmarks.

---

## Problem Statement

Agentic search systems (also called "deep research" or "search agents") interleave reasoning and web retrieval to answer complex questions. The paradigm, pioneered by Search-R1 and similar systems, trains an LLM to:
1. Formulate search queries
2. Retrieve documents
3. Reason over retrieved content
4. Iterate until confident enough to answer

Current systems treat retrieval as a **fixed oracle** — the retrieval component (BM25, DPR, or a neural ranker) is frozen, and only the reasoning agent is trained via RL. This creates a critical bottleneck: even a perfect reasoning agent cannot answer correctly if it is given irrelevant or incomplete documents.

The authors quantify this bottleneck through a controlled experiment: substituting a fixed retrieval system with an oracle retriever shows up to **+26.8% relative F1 improvement** across seven QA benchmarks. This demonstrates that the retrieval system is as important as the reasoning system for agentic search performance.

### Why Not Just Train a Better Retriever Separately?

Traditional retrieval training uses (query, relevant document) pairs with contrastive or cross-entropy losses. But in agentic search:
- Queries are **dynamically generated** by the reasoning agent during inference
- The relevance of a document depends on **what the agent has already seen and reasoned about**
- Optimal retrieval for one agent reasoning trajectory may differ from another

Standard retrieval training cannot capture these trajectory-dependent relevance signals. A jointly trained system can.

## Core Concepts & Theory

### Agentic Search as a Sequential Decision Process

Agentic search can be modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP):

```
State s_t = (question q, history h_t)  
           where h_t = {(query_i, docs_i, reasoning_i)}_{i<t}

Action a_t = reasoning step, which may include:
           - "search[query_t]" → trigger retrieval
           - "answer[final_ans]" → terminate

Transition = execute action, update history

Reward R = 1 if final_ans is correct, 0 otherwise
```

The agent policy `π_θ(a_t | s_t)` is trained via GRPO to maximize `E[R]`.

### The Ranker's Optimization Challenge

The document ranker takes a sub-query `q_t` (generated by the agent during step `t`) and ranks a set of candidate documents `{d_1, ..., d_K}`. The challenge for training the ranker via RL is:

**Group formation**: GRPO requires groups of "equivalent" inputs to normalize rewards. For the reasoning agent, all trajectories start from the same question `q`. For the ranker, each step has a **different sub-query** — so groups cannot be naively formed by question identity.

### Semantic Grouping for Ranker Training

CoSearch's key technical innovation is **semantic grouping**: clustering sub-queries generated across different trajectories by token-level similarity to form valid GRPO optimization groups. Sub-queries that are semantically similar (e.g., "capital of France" vs. "What is France's capital?") are grouped together, enabling the ranker to receive meaningful comparative reward signals.

### Composite Reward for the Ranker

The ranker is trained with a **composite reward** combining:
1. **Ranking quality signals**: How well does the top-ranked document answer the sub-query? (immediate feedback)
2. **Trajectory-level outcome**: Does the overall trajectory eventually arrive at the correct answer? (long-term feedback)

```
R_ranker(d, q_t, trajectory) = α · R_immediate(d, q_t) 
                               + (1-α) · R_outcome(final_ans)
```

This connects the ranker's decisions to the ultimate goal of answering the question correctly, preventing it from optimizing for retrieval metrics that are disconnected from downstream success.

## Main Ideas & Key Contributions

### 1. Identification of the Retrieval Bottleneck

The paper's first contribution is the empirical measurement of the retrieval gap (+26.8% relative F1 with oracle retrieval), which motivates the joint training approach and is the clearest argument for why fixed retrieval is insufficient.

### 2. Joint Reasoning-Ranking Architecture

CoSearch trains two models jointly:
- **Reasoning agent**: An LLM that plans search strategies and reasons over retrieved content
- **Generative document ranker**: A model that, given a sub-query and candidate documents, generates a ranking of the documents ordered by relevance

Both models are trained end-to-end via GRPO, sharing the trajectory-level outcome reward.

### 3. Semantic Grouping Strategy

A novel technique for forming valid GRPO groups for the ranker, which is the key algorithmic contribution enabling ranker training within the GRPO framework.

### 4. Ablation-Validated Design

Each component—semantic grouping, composite reward, joint training—is validated through ablation studies showing that removing any component degrades performance.

## Methodology & Implementation

### System Architecture

```
Question q
    ↓
Reasoning Agent (LLM)
    ├─ "search[query_t]" → Candidate Retrieval (e.g., BM25)
    │                              ↓
    │                     Generative Ranker
    │                              ↓
    │                     Top-K Re-ranked Documents
    │                              ↓
    └─ Read docs, reason, next step
    
    ...repeat until...
    
    "answer[final_answer]"
```

### Datasets Used

| Dataset | Type | # Questions |
|---------|------|------------|
| HotpotQA | Multi-hop QA | 90K train |
| 2WikiMultiHopQA | Multi-hop QA | 167K train |
| MuSiQue | Multi-hop QA | 20K train |
| NaturalQuestions | Single-hop QA | 79K train |
| TriviaQA | Single-hop QA | 78K train |
| PopQA | Single-hop QA | 14K |
| WebQ | Single-hop QA | 3K |

### Training Details

- **Base model**: A 7B or 13B LLM for the reasoning agent
- **Ranker**: A smaller model (e.g., T5-based or similar) for generative ranking
- **RL algorithm**: GRPO
- **Retrieval corpus**: Wikipedia (for all 7 QA benchmarks)
- **Candidate retrieval**: BM25 top-100 candidates, re-ranked by CoSearch ranker

### Results Summary

CoSearch achieves consistent improvement over strong baselines (including Search-R1 with fixed retrieval) across all 7 benchmarks, with especially large gains on multi-hop QA tasks where retrieval quality is most critical.

## Practical Applications & Real-World Use Cases

### 1. Enterprise Search and Knowledge Management
CoSearch's joint training paradigm could power enterprise search systems that handle complex, multi-step queries over internal document repositories. The adaptive ranker learns to surface the most relevant documents for each step of a complex research workflow.

### 2. Scientific Literature Review
Automated scientific review systems that need to synthesize dozens of papers on a topic could use CoSearch to jointly optimize the literature search strategy and synthesis reasoning.

### 3. Legal Research
Legal research often requires multi-hop reasoning (e.g., finding precedents that apply to a specific statute that relates to a specific case). CoSearch's joint optimization of retrieval and reasoning could dramatically improve automated legal research tools.

### 4. Customer Support Automation
Complex customer support queries often require looking up multiple pieces of information from product manuals, policy documents, and prior case histories. Joint reasoning+retrieval training ensures the system finds the most relevant information at each step.

## Insights & Implications

### Beyond "Frozen RAG"

The vast majority of current RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) systems treat retrieval as a fixed component, while only the generator is trained or prompted. CoSearch demonstrates that **retrieval quality is a fundamental bottleneck** and that end-to-end co-optimization is the right direction.

### The Feedback Loop Between Reasoning and Retrieval

One of the most interesting insights from CoSearch is that reasoning and retrieval are fundamentally interdependent in agentic settings: the queries the agent formulates depend on its reasoning trajectory, and the documents retrieved shape its next reasoning step. Treating these as independent systems misses this feedback loop.

### Limitations

- **Computational cost**: Joint training requires simultaneously optimizing two models, increasing training cost
- **Dependency on RL stability**: GRPO can be unstable in early training; careful hyperparameter tuning is required
- **Generalization to new domains**: The ranker is trained on specific corpora; performance on new domains (e.g., specialized scientific literature) may require domain-specific fine-tuning
